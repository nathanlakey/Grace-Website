## About

About

At 27, Grace Lakey's whole life changed with an Ancestry DNA test she took for fun. It revealed a world-champion auctioneer dad she never knew she had, and set off a chain reaction: she left corporate America for the unconventional world of auctioneering, and never looked back.

Grace's work goes way beyond her *mad cool* bid calling skills. She consults with fundraising committees on strategy, emcees events that keep people in their seats (or jumping to their feet in a good way), and brings the kind of energy that turns a quiet ballroom into a crowd eager to participate. When you see her tagline, "Add a little sparkle, raise a lot of money," know that she means every word. And yes, she can sing, too.

Grace is a licensed auctioneer. She teaches at three of the largest auction schools in the country, competes in bid calling competitions, and co-owns AuctionStream, where she hosts weekly live auctions on Whatnot and TikTok with her business partner Gracie.

Off the clock, Grace soaks in time with her (now much larger) family and travels around the world with her husband, Nate, and their fur-baby, Lady Bird. She is all about chasing dreams and making the most out of life.

Ready to raise the bar and the paddles? Let's talk.

## FAQ

FAQs

We've always used a volunteer or board member as our auctioneer. Why pay for one? Most people don't realize there's a bidder psychology behind auctions, and even more so behind benefit auctions. Fewer than 300 auctioneers in the country specialize in fundraising, which matters because trained fundraising auctioneers, like me, bring in significantly more on average than volunteer-run auctions. Through experience and education, I have the skills and proven methods to hold a room so people want to interact with me and keep their paddles in the air.

What's a paddle raise? Also called a mission moment or special appeal. Guests donate at set levels (think $5,000, $1,000, $500) with no item attached, just impact. Done right, it's often the single biggest revenue moment of the night.

What do you charge? Typically a flat fee, quoted after we talk through your event and what you need. For smaller organizations, sometimes a percentage model works better, and I'm open to it. Either way, the cost of my services should be a drop in the bucket compared to what we raise.

How far out should we book you? Most committees book 6 to 12 months ahead. If your event is sooner than that, reach out anyway - we may be able to make it work.

Do you travel? Yes. I'm based in Texas and go wherever the event is. Travel costs are quoted up front.

Our event is small. Is it worth it? Smaller rooms are some of my favorites. Fewer guests means every bidder matters more, and strategy matters most. Let's talk before you decide you're too small.

Do you handle clerking, bid-spotting, online bidding, silent auction software, or checkout? I can facilitate all of it for an additional fee: clerking, mobile bidding platforms, silent auction software, and a checkout process that doesn't leave your guests standing in line. I also have relationships with trip consignment companies and benefit auction software providers, so I can walk your committee through the options, what they cost, and what actually fits your event.
